10 On-Page SEO Techniques That Tripled Traffic in 90 Days

📅 Jun 12, 2025
⏱ 8 min read
✍️ Divyam Jain

I audited 12 client sites over 90 days and tracked every on-page change against traffic movement. What followed was a clear pattern: the same handful of techniques consistently moved rankings, and most sites were ignoring all of them. Here's what actually worked.

1. Fix Your Title Tags — They're Probably Weak

The single highest-leverage on-page change I made across every site. Most businesses write title tags like they're naming a page in their own filing system — descriptive but not compelling. Google uses the title tag as the primary signal for what a page is about, and users use it to decide whether to click.

The formula that consistently outperformed: Primary Keyword + Specific Benefit + Brand Name. Keep it under 60 characters. Front-load the keyword. Make it sound like something a person would actually want to read.

Example: "Services - Acme Legal" became "Commercial Property Lawyer in Mumbai | Acme Legal". Traffic to that page increased 180% in six weeks.

2. Write Meta Descriptions That Get Clicked

Meta descriptions don’t directly affect rankings, but they dramatically affect click-through rate — which does. A higher CTR sends positive signals to Google and means more visitors without any change in ranking position. Treat the meta description as ad copy, not a summary.

  • Include the primary keyword naturally
  • State a specific benefit or outcome
  • Add a soft call-to-action at the end
  • Keep it 140–160 characters

3. H1 Once, H2s as Subtopics, Never Decorative

Your heading structure is how Google understands the hierarchy of your content. One H1 per page, containing your primary keyword. H2s that represent the genuine subtopics of the page. H3s for sub-points within those sections. Never use headings just to make text look big.

I found sites consistently using H2s for visual styling rather than content structure. Fixing this alone produced measurable ranking improvements within three to four weeks across five client sites.

4. The Internal Linking Opportunity Most Sites Miss

Every internal link is a signal about what matters on your site. Most businesses have valuable pages buried three or four clicks from the homepage with almost no internal links pointing at them. Google sees this and concludes those pages aren’t important.

The fix: audit which pages you want to rank, count how many internal links they receive, and add contextual links from relevant existing content. This is one of the cheapest, fastest ways to push pages up in rankings without any new content or backlinks.

5. Optimise for Search Intent, Not Just the Keyword

You can rank for a keyword and still lose to competitors if your page doesn’t match what the person searching actually wants. Google classifies search intent into four categories: informational, navigational, commercial, and transactional. Your content needs to match the dominant intent for your target keyword.

Practical check: Google your target keyword and look at the top 5 results. Are they blog posts? Product pages? Comparison articles? Whatever they are, your page needs to be that type of content.

6. Image Alt Text That Actually Helps Rankings

Alt text exists for accessibility, but Google reads it as descriptive context for your images. Most businesses either leave alt text blank or stuff it with keywords. The right approach: write a genuine description of what the image shows, include the relevant keyword only where it fits naturally.

7. Page Speed: The Ranking Factor Hiding in Plain Sight

Core Web Vitals have been a confirmed ranking factor since 2021. Largest Contentful Paint, Cumulative Layout Shift, and Interaction to Next Paint are measurable, improvable, and often dramatically worse than site owners realise. Run PageSpeed Insights on your homepage right now. If you’re below 70 on mobile, this is your highest-priority fix.

8. Schema Markup for Rich Results

Schema markup tells Google exactly what type of content a page contains. The right schema can earn you rich results — star ratings, FAQs, event dates — that take up more real estate in search results and dramatically increase click-through rates. For service businesses, I recommend Organisation, LocalBusiness, and Service schema as a starting point.

9. Update Stale Content Before Creating New Content

New content gets all the glory, but your existing content is often sitting on accumulated authority that’s being wasted. Pages ranking on page two or three for good keywords are often there because the content is outdated, shallow, or doesn’t fully cover the topic. Updating these pages consistently outperforms creating new pages targeting the same keywords.

10. Reduce Click Depth on Your Most Important Pages

Click depth — the number of clicks required to reach a page from your homepage — affects how frequently Googlebot crawls it and how much authority it receives. Important pages buried in subfolder structures three or four levels deep get crawled less often and earn less link equity. Flatten your site structure wherever possible and add direct navigation links to high-priority pages.

The short version: On-page SEO is about giving Google clear, consistent signals about what each page covers and why it deserves to rank. Most sites are sending mixed or weak signals. Clean them up and the rankings follow.
