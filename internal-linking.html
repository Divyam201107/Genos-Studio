Internal Linking: The Most Underrated SEO Tactic

📅 Mar 22, 2025
⏱ 6 min read
✍️ Divyam Jain

If you had to name one SEO improvement that costs nothing, requires no new content, and consistently produces ranking improvements within 4–8 weeks — internal linking is it. And almost every site I audit is doing it poorly or not at all.

What Internal Links Actually Do

Every link on your website passes what's called "link equity" (or "PageRank") — a signal of authority — to the page it links to. When external websites link to your homepage, your homepage accumulates authority. Internal links then distribute that authority throughout your site.

The problem: most sites have all their link equity pooled in the homepage, with thin trickles reaching the pages that actually matter for conversions — service pages, landing pages, specific blog posts targeting valuable keywords.

The simple version: Your homepage is popular. Your services page needs to rank. Link from your homepage to your services page, and from relevant blog posts to your services page. You're passing earned authority to the pages that need it.

The Three Internal Linking Mistakes I See Constantly

  • Linking only from the navigation. Nav links matter, but Google devalues them slightly because they appear on every page and are seen as structural rather than editorial. Contextual links — links within the body of your content — carry much more weight.
  • Using generic anchor text. "Click here" and "Read more" tell Google nothing about what the linked page is about. Use descriptive anchor text that includes the keyword the target page is trying to rank for. "My SEO strategy service" is infinitely better than "learn more."
  • Forgetting to update old content. Every time you publish a new page, you should go back through relevant old content and add contextual links to the new page. Most people never do this — they just publish and move on.

How to Audit Your Internal Links Right Now

Open Google Search Console, go to the Links report, and look at Internal links. You'll see which pages receive the most internal links. If your contact page or homepage dominate the list and your key service pages are barely there — that's the problem, and it's fixable this week.

The Practical Process I Use

  • Identify the 5–10 pages most important to your business — the ones you want to rank
  • For each of those pages, identify the primary keyword it targets
  • Search your entire site for existing content that mentions topics related to that keyword
  • Add a contextual link from that existing content to the target page using the keyword as anchor text
  • Repeat for each target page, aiming for at least 3–5 contextual internal links per important page

This is not complicated. It doesn't require any new content. It doesn't require any technical work. It just requires going through your site systematically and connecting the dots. I've seen pages jump from position 14 to position 6 within six weeks from internal linking alone — no new content, no backlinks, just better distribution of existing authority.
